Bacancy Nutrition Guide - Seasonal Fruits Handbook
Published: January 2025
Department: Employee Wellness Program

1. Apple

Apples are one of the most widely consumed fruits globally and are available year-round in most regions. The most popular varieties include Fuji, Gala, Granny Smith, Honeycrisp, and Red Delicious. Each variety has a distinct flavor profile ranging from sweet to tart.

Nutritional value per medium apple (182g): 95 calories, 25g carbohydrates, 4.4g dietary fiber, 19g natural sugars, and 14% of daily Vitamin C requirement. Apples are also rich in polyphenols and quercetin, which act as antioxidants.

Storage guidelines: Store apples in the refrigerator crisper drawer to maintain freshness for up to 6 weeks. At room temperature, apples last approximately 1 week. Keep them separate from other fruits as apples release ethylene gas which accelerates ripening of nearby produce.

Health benefits: Regular apple consumption has been linked to reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, improved gut health due to pectin fiber content, and better blood sugar regulation. The skin contains the highest concentration of fiber and antioxidants, so consuming unpeeled apples is recommended.

Best season: September through November for peak freshness and flavor, though cold storage makes them available throughout the year.

2. Orange

Oranges are citrus fruits known for their high Vitamin C content and refreshing juice. Major varieties include Navel, Valencia, Blood Orange, and Cara Cara. Navel oranges are best for eating fresh while Valencia oranges are preferred for juicing.

Nutritional value per medium orange (131g): 62 calories, 15g carbohydrates, 3.1g dietary fiber, 12g natural sugars, and 116% of daily Vitamin C requirement. Oranges also provide folate, thiamine, and potassium.

Storage guidelines: Oranges can be stored at room temperature for up to 1 week or refrigerated for up to 3 weeks. Pre-cut orange segments should be refrigerated and consumed within 3 days. Orange zest can be dried and stored in airtight containers for up to 6 months.

Health benefits: The high Vitamin C content supports immune function and iron absorption. Oranges contain hesperidin and naringenin, flavonoids that support heart health and reduce inflammation. The fiber content aids digestion and promotes satiety. Drinking fresh orange juice provides quick hydration but lacks the fiber benefits of whole fruit consumption.

Best season: December through April for Navel oranges, March through July for Valencia oranges.

3. Mango

Mangoes are tropical stone fruits often called the "king of fruits." Popular varieties include Alphonso (India), Kent (Americas), Tommy Atkins (commercial), and Ataulfo (honey mango). Alphonso mangoes from India are considered the most flavorful and aromatic variety worldwide.

Nutritional value per cup of sliced mango (165g): 99 calories, 25g carbohydrates, 2.6g dietary fiber, 23g natural sugars, 67% of daily Vitamin C, and 10% of daily Vitamin A. Mangoes are one of the richest sources of beta-carotene among fruits.

Storage guidelines: Unripe mangoes should be kept at room temperature until they yield slightly to gentle pressure and emit a fruity aroma at the stem end. Once ripe, refrigerate for up to 5 days. Cut mango can be frozen for up to 6 months. To speed ripening, place mango in a paper bag with a banana.

Health benefits: Mangoes support eye health due to high Vitamin A and zeaxanthin content. They contain digestive enzymes called amylases that help break down complex carbohydrates. The polyphenols in mango have been studied for anti-cancer properties. Mango skin contains urushiol, the same compound found in poison ivy, which can cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals.

Best season: April through August, varying by variety and growing region.

4. Blueberry

Blueberries are small berries native to North America and are classified as a superfood due to their exceptional antioxidant content. Varieties include highbush (most commercial), lowbush (wild), and rabbiteye. Wild blueberries are smaller but contain significantly higher antioxidant levels than cultivated varieties.

Nutritional value per cup (148g): 84 calories, 21g carbohydrates, 3.6g dietary fiber, 15g natural sugars, 24% of daily Vitamin C, and 36% of daily Vitamin K. Blueberries have the highest antioxidant capacity among commonly consumed fruits and vegetables.

Storage guidelines: Do not wash blueberries until ready to eat as moisture promotes mold growth. Refrigerate in a single layer on a paper towel-lined container for up to 10 days. Frozen blueberries retain nutritional value for up to 12 months. Remove any damaged berries immediately to prevent spoilage from spreading.

Health benefits: Blueberries contain anthocyanins, the pigments responsible for their blue color, which are powerful antioxidants linked to improved memory and cognitive function. Regular consumption has been associated with reduced blood pressure, lower LDL cholesterol oxidation, and improved insulin sensitivity. Studies suggest eating 1 cup daily may reduce cognitive decline by up to 2.5 years.

Best season: June through August for fresh, available frozen year-round.

5. Kiwi

Kiwifruit (also called Chinese gooseberry) originated in China and was commercialized in New Zealand. The two main varieties are green kiwi (Hayward) with fuzzy brown skin and gold kiwi (SunGold) with smooth skin and sweeter flavor. Gold kiwi has nearly three times the Vitamin C content of green kiwi.

Nutritional value per medium green kiwi (69g): 42 calories, 10g carbohydrates, 2.1g dietary fiber, 6g natural sugars, 71% of daily Vitamin C, and 23% of daily Vitamin K. Kiwi contains more Vitamin C per gram than most citrus fruits. It also provides actinidin, a natural enzyme that aids protein digestion.

Storage guidelines: Firm kiwis can be stored at room temperature for up to 4 weeks or refrigerated for up to 8 weeks. To ripen quickly, place in a paper bag with an apple or banana. Once ripe (yields to gentle pressure), consume within 3 days. The skin of gold kiwi is edible and provides additional fiber.

Health benefits: Kiwi is one of the most nutrient-dense fruits available. Eating 2 kiwis before bedtime has been shown to improve sleep quality and duration due to high serotonin and antioxidant content. The actinidin enzyme helps digest protein-rich meals. Kiwi consumption supports respiratory health and has been linked to reduced severity of cold and flu symptoms. The high Vitamin K content supports bone health and blood clotting.

Best season: November through May for green kiwi, May through January for gold kiwi.

7. Storage Quick Reference

Room temperature storage duration:
- Apple: 1 week
- Orange: 1 week
- Mango (ripe): consume within 2 days
- Blueberry: not recommended, refrigerate immediately
- Kiwi (firm): up to 4 weeks

Refrigerator storage duration:
- Apple: up to 6 weeks
- Orange: up to 3 weeks
- Blueberry: up to 10 days
- Mango (ripe): up to 5 days
- Kiwi (firm): up to 8 weeks
